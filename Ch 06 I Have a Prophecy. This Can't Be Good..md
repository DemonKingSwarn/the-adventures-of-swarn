# Chapter 6: I Have a Prophecy. This Can't Be Good.

The interior of the cabin was made of dark blue marble, with a shivling at the center of the cabin.

It felt lonely. I had gotten used to the overcrowded Narad cabin, with everyone barely getting a place to sit down. The shivling had some Sanskrit carvings on it.

The carvings glowed faintly when I looked at them for too long. I never tried to read them. Some things you just don't want to know.

Sujan and Ria visited me from time to time during the day breaks. It wasn't the same as living with them, but it kept me sane.

Later that night, I couldn't sleep. I was lying on my bed, staring at the ceiling.

That's when I walked out of the cabin to give Astrid a visit. I sat down under her tree.

"So we both were never supposed to be born," I said.

"Did you know about this?" I asked softly. "I guess, if they already knew you were Vishnu's daughter," I added.

"Sujan told me you never liked your father Vishnu," I said, slowly.

I just sat there under her for a long moment. Then I noticed Aryan coming towards me.

"Hey, you couldn't sleep either?" I said.

"No, we Bhutas rarely sleep. I came to see you. Thought you might need a friend after that entire claiming ordeal," Aryan said.

"Thanks, buddy. I was just kinda talking to Astrid. I mean, you know, we both being the forbidden child and all. Though it's one-sided," I said, slowly.

"I know. I was the one who accompanied Sujan, Ria, and Astrid to camp. I failed my duty when I let Astrid die—well, technically she got turned into this tree. But you get what I mean," Aryan added.

He couldn't look at the tree while he said it. He looked at the ground, like even the soil under it blamed him.

"Wait, they came to camp with you? Like I did?" I asked.

Aryan nodded.

"You were a kid too," I said. "And you got them here." He didn't answer that.

"Anyway, I will see you soon. I have something to do tonight," Aryan said and left.

I leaned back against Astrid's tree again and looked up at the leaves and branches.

"I wish you were here with me," I said, softly.

…

That night, I dreamed of the hooded man again. He had started to become a constant at this point.

"So now you know who your father is, little hero," he said in a raspy voice. "You have been lied to by the gods. You are born to do so much more," he added.

"Who are you?" I asked him again, with force this time.

To which he chuckled again and just left. But this time, before he left, I caught a glimpse of gold on his scythe.

…

The next morning I was called to the big house, in front of both Vajivega and Varuni this time. They explained that the spirit of Akashvani wanted to speak to me.

I entered the attic of the big house.

The attic was filled with a lot of things and was really dusty, as if it had never been cleaned. There was also my Mahishasura horn there. The weirdest thing I saw there was this weird-looking blade made out of bones.

Dust floated in the single beam of light from the window. Everything in there felt like a memory someone had packed away and forgotten—except the bone blade. The bone blade felt like a warning.

Then my gaze dropped to a mummified body, which I knew was the Akashvani because of what they had explained to me.

I bowed respectfully and said, "Hey, I am Swarn. I have been told that you wanted to speak with me."

Nothing happened.

Then I said nervously, "I think you are busy. I will come later." And I turned around.

But then I heard it standing up, with bones creaking.
I turned to face it, and green smoke started to come out of its mouth.
And then I saw a holographic image of someone I hate—my step-dad.

Of all the faces in the world, why his? I had no answer. I still don't.

"You shall go where no demigod has returned from,
You shall face the god who has been turned.
Fail to save what matters most,
And you shall be betrayed by the one you trust the most," it said in a raspy voice.

As soon as the green smoke and the image faded and the body fell back down onto the chair, I rushed outside the attic.

My knees had gone weak somewhere between the second and third line. I didn't decide to run. My legs decided for me.

…

I told Vajivega and Varuni the entire prophecy verbatim, which made them wonder too.

"Gather the camp," said Vajivega. "It's time to announce a quest," he added.

We all went to the amphitheatre, with me, Vajivega, and Varuni standing at the center of it.

"The quest will lead you to Dandakaranya. And you will face whatever evils you have to face," Vajivega said.

"You may choose two who will accompany you on this quest," said Varuni.

I looked at all the campers, and the words of the prophecy were echoing in my mind: "You shall be betrayed by the one you trust the most."

I found Sujan first. He gave me a small nod, like, go on. I looked at Sanvi, who glared back. And then I looked at the only two people in that crowd who had never once made me feel like a problem.

"I choose Ria and Aryan to go with me," I said.

…

I sat at the edge of the shivling in my Shiva cabin while packing my bag. That's when Sujan came to visit me.

He didn't say much at first. He just took one look at my bag, sighed, and started re-packing it properly, because apparently I pack like a disaster.

I looked at Sujan and said, "Uhm, sorry about not choosing you. It's just, the Akashvani said…"

"It's okay, I am not mad about it," Sujan replied. "Looks like Vajivega gave you quite a lot to pack," he added.

"Oh, he gave me some money, and I don't know what these are—some kind of coin, I guess," I said.

"Those are Nishka. Money for the human world, Nishka for the mythical world," Sujan replied.

"Here, take these," Sujan said.

He handed me a box and said, "This should come in handy during your quest."

I opened it and saw a pair of shoes with wings forming from their shoelaces.

"They are something," I said.

"They're from my father," Sujan replied. "I don't need them," he added.

From what I had learned from Sujan so far, even he didn't like his father much, so I didn't question it.

He stayed a while longer. We didn't talk about the prophecy, or the quest, or the betrayal line. We just sat on the steps of the Shiva cabin, and for a little while it felt like being back in the Narad cabin. Before he left, he gripped my shoulder and said, "Come back in one piece, okay? You're the closest thing I have to a little brother." I promised him I would. I meant it.

I walked out with him and noticed Sanvi and the other campers eyeing me.

"Why are they looking at me?" I asked Sujan.

"Having a quest from the gods means glory," Sujan replied.

"So they are jealous?" I asked.

"Yeah, you can say that," Sujan replied.
