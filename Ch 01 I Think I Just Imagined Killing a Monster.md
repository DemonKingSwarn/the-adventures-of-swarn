# Chapter 1: I Think I Just Imagined Killing a Monster

I woke up from my power nap on the bus feeling like I had slept for hours. I looked around and noticed everyone was getting off. Today we were on a field trip to a museum about Hindu Mythology, one of the only topics I liked, for some reason.

Oh, you don't know me yet. I am Swarn. I am twelve years old, and I am a troubled kid. I have, like, switched three schools in three years. And I got a step-dad at home who is an asshole. I feel bad for my mom, who has to live with him alone while I am at the dorm at Pallavi High. I have got both dyslexia and ADHD—yeah, I am weird like that.

Ever since I was a kid, I have seen things most people couldn't. Like that one time there was this flying horse on the roof of the building opposite my classroom. I pointed it out to the kid sitting next to me, and by the time he looked, it was gone. He never sat next to me again. After enough of those, you learn to keep what you see to yourself.

The museum itself was this huge old building with high ceilings and cold stone floors, every hall filled with statues of gods and heroes. Everyone else looked bored out of their minds, but for some reason I felt weirdly at home there, like the statues were watching me walk by. It should have creeped me out. It didn't.

I was standing with the other kids in my class group in front of a statue of a Hindu hero when Alison Hogan, the class bully, threw a paper ball at me that hit my head. She and her friends started laughing.

I picked the paper ball off the floor and crushed it in my fist. Three schools in three years, and every single one of them had an Alison Hogan.

"That's enough!" Mr Sharma said as he moved his wheelchair towards me.

Mr Sharma looked at me empathetically and said, "Swarn, would you like to join us in the class? Tell me, who is the hero in this statue?"

I looked at the statue, and then at the Sanskrit carvings on it. For some reason, I can easily understand Sanskrit. I focused on it and it read "Parshuram," and then I told him confidently, "It's Parshuram."

"Correct!" said Mr Sharma. "I knew you could do it," he added.

He must have noticed that I had lost my pen, because he came closer to me and gave me one of his cool-looking ballpoint pens.
"Take care of it. It's a mighty weapon," Mr Sharma said.

I laughed a little, because it was just a pen. But the way he looked at me when he said it, like he was being completely serious, made me stop. I slipped it into my pocket anyway. "Thanks, Mr Sharma."

…

It was the lunch break and we all got sandwiches. I was sitting with my only friend here, who is also my best friend. His name is Aryan, and he is also at the bottom of the food chain, like me. Also, he kinda has this leg injury that makes it uncomfortable for him to walk or sit, and he always wears full pants to hide his legs.

Aryan traded me his cucumber sandwich for my cheese one, like he always did, and for a little while things were okay. Normal, even. I should have known it wouldn't last.

We were talking and having fun when Alison Hogan threw a cheese slice into Aryan's hair. He is always scared of bullies, and he said slowly, "Ignore her. One thing I know about bullies is you should never fight them back."

I looked at him like he had gone mad and said, "Seriously? That is the last thing you should do."

So I stood up for him. The walk to her table felt like it took forever, my ears ringing the whole way, and as soon as I reached for her, something happened and I blacked out. But for a brief moment of consciousness, I could feel cool water. Not on my skin—inside my chest, like something in me had stretched out and grabbed it.

When I opened my eyes, Alison Hogan was in the fountain, but from what I remembered, I never even got to touch her, let alone push her into the fountain.
And she started screaming, "Swarn hit me, he pushed me into this fountain!"

Everyone was staring at me. A few kids already had their phones out. I looked down at my own hands. They were completely dry.

Then suddenly I felt someone grab my hand. It was Mrs. Shristi, our substitute teacher, who always hated me and loved her oh-so-precious Alison Hogan. So I knew I was in trouble for sure now. She dragged me out of the group while Aryan looked at me with panicked eyes.

Her grip was way too strong for a substitute teacher. She pulled me through hall after hall, past the statues, past the other school groups, and the weirdest thing is that nobody even looked up at us. Not one teacher. Not one guard. It was like we were invisible.

She took me to a secluded area in the museum.

"Hey, uhm, Mrs. Shristi, I didn't push her into that fountain," I said pleadingly.

"You thought we couldn't find you? That we wouldn't know who your father is?" she said.

It confused me, and I replied, "What about him? Is it about my step-dad? Did he call or something?"

"Oh, you little thing, don't you play with me," she said. And as she spoke, her body turned fully black, with leathery bat wings coming out of her, and then she lunged at me.

I want to tell you I did something heroic. I didn't. I just stood there, frozen, my brain still stuck on "this isn't real," while she came at me with claws where her fingers should have been.

I was on the floor with her on top of me, but before she could hurt me, the pen Mr Sharma gave me grew hot in my pocket, and it turned into something big, like a staff with three prongs or something—heavy, bronze, and humming like it was alive. My body moved before my brain could catch up, and the last thing I remember, she was turning to dust. Golden dust, settling all over my shoes.

I stood up and looked at my hand, and the pen was—surprise, surprise—a pen. "Uhm, what just happened here? Is anyone here? Mrs. Shristi?" I said.

Nobody answered. The hall was empty and quiet, like nothing had happened at all. My hands wouldn't stop shaking, so I shoved them into my pockets, where the pen was already cool again, like it hadn't just been a three-pronged monster weapon a minute ago.

I blamed it all on my ADHD, since it always makes me imagine things that actually feel real. I went out of the secluded area and noticed Alison Hogan was still drenched, with some teacher I had never seen before holding a towel around her.

I approached Aryan and asked him, "Uhm, hey dude, where is Mrs. Shristi?" He looked at me like I had said something really funny and then replied, "Who?"

Nobody remembered her. Not the teachers, not Alison Hogan's friends, nobody. Like she had never existed at all.
