# Chapter 5: Well, That Was One Way to Get Claimed

Later that night, the hooded man with the scythe returned in my dreams. But this time, he spoke to me. "You are going to be the key, little hero," he said.

"Who are you?" I screamed.

To which he just chuckled, like I had told him a joke. Ria had told me that dreams, to demigods like us, are kind of like a message or a glimpse of the future, so I wondered what this dream could mean.

I woke up freezing, even though the cabin was warm. The cold from his voice stayed in my bones till morning.

The next morning, we gathered up for Capture the Flag. Vajivega started speaking. "The rules of Capture the Flag are: no killing or maiming, and no going outside the boundaries of the camp. Now start." He blew into a sea snail's giant shell to signal that it had begun. Don't ask how I know whose shell it is—I just know, for some reason.

Sujan, Ria, and I were on the same team. Sujan took half the team with him, while Ria and I went together.

"So, what do you think of last night?" I asked.

"I have some theories, but I would need some time to be sure of it. I am, like, 90% sure right now," Ria replied.

Seeing how sure she was of it, I felt like I was the only one who couldn't see the answer that had been right in front of me all this time.

…

She and I went to guard our flag, alone. I lay there on the rock while she strategised our every move.

She then looked at me curiously and asked, "Are you going to just lie there like we are on a picnic, or help me?"

I chuckled.

But then Sanvi and her gang circled us out of nowhere, and Sanvi walked forward with her spear.

"Let's see if you can handle this," Sanvi said, mockingly.

"Isn't 'no maiming' one rule you can't break?" I said.

"Oh, I know. I might lose dessert privileges for some time," Sanvi replied.

Sanvi lunged at me, and I immediately uncapped my pen and used the trident to block her spear. Damn, she's good. I guess it makes sense—she is the daughter of the war god.

We exchanged blows. She fought like her spear was part of her body—jab, sweep, feint—and I gave ground step after step, blocking whatever I could, my arms already aching. She landed another strike at me, which Ria blocked using her dagger. And then I somehow made the ground below us shake, and two of Sanvi's friends fell.

But then I had the same dancing sensation again, and this time I felt something stronger in my forehead. It was like the bathroom all over again, except bigger. My body knew moves I had never learned. I suddenly became a lot more aggressive, and my body felt like it had just been refuelled with energy. I lunged forward and easily broke Sanvi's spear in half.

The two halves spun away into the grass. Sanvi stared at the broken shaft in her hands like she didn't understand what it was anymore.

Sanvi screamed at me and was about to lunge forward.

But before it could happen, the sound of shells could be heard again, indicating the Capture the Flag had ended.

Then I looked at Ria, and she was looking above me.

"What happened?" I asked her.

"Your dad's calling!" Ria replied.

And I looked up, and a big holographic green trident was above me. It hung there, glowing, turning slowly, and the whole camp had gone dead silent. Then I looked back at her, but she and every other camper—including even Vajivega—kneeled. All of them. Even Sanvi, slowly, like her knees were doing it against her will.

"You have been claimed by Shiva—Nataraj, the Destroyer, Kaal Bhairav, Lord of Animals. Swarn, the son of Shiva," Vajivega said.

Twelve years of being told I was broken, weird, too much. And now the sky itself was saying whose son I was. I should have felt happy. Mostly I felt terrified—and, for the first time in my life, seen.

And suddenly I was not the unclaimed demigod living in Narad's cabin anymore. I was Swarn, the son of Shiva.

Sujan was grinning at me from the kneeling crowd like I had just won something. I didn't feel like I had won anything.

"As the forbidden child of the destroyer god, you will stay in the Shiva cabin," Vajivega added.

The moment I entered the Shiva cabin, I noticed how quiet it was. There were literally no other demigods. Just like the Vishnu cabin, since his only daughter Astrid is now a peepal tree, and the Brahma cabin.

My footsteps echoed. In the Narad cabin, you couldn't hear yourself think. Here, I could hear my own breathing. My little bag of stuff sat in the corner, looking tiny. That first night, I missed the overcrowded Narad cabin more than I could explain. I missed my mom more than I could bear.

I turned to Vajivega and asked, "Why is it that all the big three cabins and the Parvati cabin are empty?"

To which he replied, with a little hesitancy, "It is because, long ago, Shiva, Vishnu, and Brahma heard a prophecy that either Vishnu's or Shiva's child would be a problem in the future. Which is why they swore an oath to never sire any demigod children." And the whole time he said it, he never once looked me in the eyes.

The words landed somewhere deep in my chest. A problem. I hadn't just been unclaimed for a week. I was never supposed to exist at all.

"You didn't tell me why the Parvati cabin is empty," I said.

"It's because she doesn't have any demigod children. We offer her a cabin here at camp as respect," Vajivega replied.
