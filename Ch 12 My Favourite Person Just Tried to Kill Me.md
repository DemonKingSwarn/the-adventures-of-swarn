# Chapter 12: My Favourite Person Just Tried to Kill Me

Later that night, when everyone was celebrating. Sujan took me for a little walk to where we always trained.

I went without thinking twice. This was Sujan. This was the place where he had taught me to hold a trident, knocked me down a hundred times, and pulled me back up almost as many. After everything that had happened on the quest, walking there with him felt almost normal.

“I heard you fought Kartikeya during your quest.” Sujan said.

“Yeah, it was more of an instinct.” I replied. “I did rely a lot on your training.”

“There’s one more thing, uhm I lost the shoes you gave me when I was at Dandakaranya. They acted  like they had the minds of their own and started dragging me towards the lake.” I said slowly, hesitantly.

Though Sujan didn’t look surprised at all, he looked like he was expecting that to happen.

That should have warned me. Instead, I waited for him to explain. He was Sujan. If something was wrong, there had to be a reason, and he was going to tell me what it was.

“Wait! The prophecy said…” I hesitated the bit.

“You shall go where no demigod has returned from” I spoke softly.

“Your journey to Dandakaranya.” Sujan replied.

“You shall face the god who has been turned.” I spoke again.

“Kartikeya” Sujan replied.

“Fail to save what matters most.” I said.

“It could only mean my mother at Yama’s court whom I had to leave behind at that time.”

Sujan nodded.

My mouth had gone dry. I didn't want to say the last line. Saying it out loud felt like it would make it true. Sujan just stood there, watching me understand.

“And you shall be betrayed by the one you trust the most.” I said.

“It was you!” I said with sadness in my voice as I finally pieced together everything.

For a second, all I could see were the stupid little moments. Sujan re-packing my bag. Sujan grinning when I got claimed. His hand on my shoulder when he told me to come back in one piece. The brother I thought I had found was standing in front of me, and he had known exactly where those shoes were supposed to take me.

“I wasn’t expecting you to get out of those shoes.” Sujan said. “You could have joined my cause.”

“Your cause? What are you talking about?” I said.

“The gods have ruled for far too long, they have the ego at the size of a mountain. That’s why I joined Kāl’s forces, to overthrow them.” Sujan replied.

Suddenly I felt the same coldness I always felt whenever that hooded man spoke to me. That’s whom I was hearing from all this time, Kāl.

The scary part was I actually believed him. Though if I hadn't met my father, I might have actually joined his side.

That was what made it terrifying. He wasn't offering me something obviously evil. I had heard Ahalya's story. I had seen what Narad did for fun, and listened to Danu and Diti talk about their dead children. Sujan was using things I already knew were wrong and asking me to do something about them. A small part of me still wanted to listen.

I took out my ballpoint pen again and uncapped it. And Sujan took out his sword. It was a different blade, not made from celestial bronze which we demigods use for our weapons. Half of it was celestial bronze and the other half was normal human steel.

Sujan was quick to attack, but I managed to block it in the last moment.

Even then, some stupid part of me expected him to stop. To lower his sword and tell me this had gone too far. This was the person who had taught me that block. He knew exactly what I was going to do before I did it.

And when I lunged forward with my trident, a scorpion beat me. It wasn’t the scorpion I recognised though. I fell on the ground.

The pain came a second later, hot and sharp, and then my leg stopped feeling like it belonged to me. I tried to push myself back up and my arms folded underneath me.

“Don’t try it! It’s from Narak, no mortal has the cure for its venom.” Sujan said.

“Farewell, Swarn.” Sujan said.

I looked up at him. Not at Kāl's follower, not at the traitor from the prophecy. At Sujan. I think I was still waiting for him to change his mind.

And then he sliced his sword in the air and a rift opened through it and he went through and the rift was gone.

The person who had called me his little brother left me alone on the ground.

Every time I thought about giving up, I thought about my mom finally being home. I thought about Ria and Aryan and how I had just gotten them back. I couldn't die here, not after everything.

I struggled, but eventually started crawling towards the big house. I fell unconscious when I reached the foothills of Astrid’s peepal tree.

…

I felt someone feeding me.

I slowly opened my eyes and I see Ria feeding me Amrit and Somras.

“You are awake.” Ria said, softly. “We found you unconscious.”

I told her, Varuni, and Vajivega exactly what happened back there, and Sujan’s betrayal. It broke Ria, after all she came to camp with Sujan when she was 7 years old. It made me a lot angry.

Ria didn't say anything at first. The hand holding the cup of Somras went completely still. She looked towards the door, like she half expected Sujan to walk through it and tell her I had misunderstood. When the tears finally came, she wiped them away angrily, as if she was furious at herself for crying over him.

Nobody told her not to. Nobody told me it would be okay. We all just sat with it, because there wasn't anything else to do.

…

The next day, we got this summer’s bead for our bead necklaces, which makes it my first bead. This year it had the symbol of a trident, marking my coming to the camp.

Everyone clapped, and I tried to smile. Then I automatically looked for Sujan in the crowd, because he was the person I wanted to see it. The empty space where he should have been hurt almost as much as the scorpion sting.


Ria, me, and Aryan were standing at the Astrid’s peepal tree. Planning to leave for the outside world, and I was excited to see my mother again.

Ria decided to go home for the first time in 5 years, since she came.

She kept looking towards the camp gate like she wasn't sure she could walk through it. I understood. Going home sounds simple until home is somewhere you ran away from.

There was also one person missing from our goodbye. None of us mentioned him. Somehow that made the silence around his name even louder.

“Let’s promise to meet here next year.” I said.

“Yeah.” Ria said, smiling.

“We could be the Three Musketeers.” Aryan said.

To which all three of us laughed. And then we hugged each other and finally left camp.

I needed that laugh more than I wanted to admit. The Three Musketeers sounded ridiculous. It also sounded like a promise that the three of us were still here, and that not every person I trusted was going to disappear.

…

When I reached back my apartment at Bangalore, where we stay. I stopped at my tracks, as I just remembered by idiot step-dad.

But then right outside of our door I found a note.

“I told you I keep my side of the deal. Your mom is free, including from your step-dad. He is in my realm now, awaiting judgement from me.” 

And it was signed by Yama.

I sighed with relief and then entered through the door. I immediately went to find my mom and just hugged her.

She was warm. That was the first thing I noticed. Not golden, not frozen, not dust slipping through my fingers. Warm and alive and solid, with her arms around me.

I buried my face against her shoulder and finally started crying. All of it came out—the museum, the border, Yama's throne room, almost dying, Sujan. I had spent weeks trying to be brave because there was always someone I had to protect or somewhere I had to get to. I didn't have to do any of that right then.

My mom held me the way she used to when I was little, one hand on the back of my head, and didn't let go. I don't know how long we stood there. Long enough for the apartment to stop feeling strange. Long enough for me to believe I was actually home.

…
