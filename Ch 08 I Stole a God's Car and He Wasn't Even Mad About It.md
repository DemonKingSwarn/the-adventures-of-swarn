# Chapter 8: I Stole a God's Car and He Wasn't Even Mad About It

Our train stopped at Madhya Pradesh, so we had to continue our journey on foot again, as even the Patal shortcut was a lot further from here.

"So, where to now?" I asked.

"Well, we are still very far from our destination—even Patal," Ria replied.

That's when we saw a weird building, and it read "Narad's Abode."

"Do you guys think it's the actual god Narad, or something they just named after him?" I asked.

"Who knows? It can be either," Aryan replied.

We went inside the building, and it had so many people living inside, like a community, though something felt off.

It took me a minute to place what was wrong. The clothes. The music crackling out of an old radio. The way everyone moved, like actors in one of those old movies. Nobody was in a hurry. And nobody ever looked at the door.

"Guys, stay close. I don't feel good about this place," Ria said.

And before I could say anything to her, we noticed Aryan was already gone. We started looking for him.

My stomach dropped. We called his name. Nobody answered.

"Where do you think a stupid bhuta can go?" I asked.

"Don't know. But can you believe it? He went right when I said I didn't feel good about this place," Ria replied.

But then Ria disappeared too.

One second she was right beside me, close enough that her shoulder brushed my arm, and the next I was alone in a crowd full of people who didn't belong to my century.

…

I asked a guy who was reading a newspaper, "Hey, did you happen to see a girl who was with me a few moments ago?"

"Nope," he replied, but his gesture felt weird. Like, too formal—like the ones I see on those TV shows based on ancient times.

"Uhm, what year is this?" I asked him.

"1740, why?" he replied.

Which surprised me, because he should have been dead by now.

Something told me that this was no ordinary place. I walked a little further, where I found a middle-aged man who was acting surprisingly normal for this kind of place, but still being weird.

I approached him.

"Uhm, hello?" I said.

"Oh? Oh, it's you, kiddo—the one who is quite popular amongst us recently. It's good to finally meet you, Swarn. I am Narad," he said, smiling like a weirdo.

"What are you doing here? And what is this place?" I asked.

"Well, I am having fun. Besides, don't you know my story with the illusions of Maya? After that, I did start using it to my advantage, keeping a few mortals in here for centuries to have fun with me. To them, they are still living in their time," he replied.

"That's cruel!" I said.

"Cruel? Since when is having fun cruel?" he replied.

At that moment, I kinda saw why Sujan would hate his father.

"Where are my friends?" I asked, but unlike my earlier polite question, I was angry now.

"Oh? You want them back? Sure. But they were having fun before this; the Maya probably gave them some adventure," he said, and then snapped his fingers, and they appeared in front of us.

"Mhm," Aryan said.

"Huh? What just happened?" said Ria.

I immediately hugged them and then faced Narad with them.

"You kids are no fun. I should throw you all out right now!" Narad said.

"We are not fun? You are the one who kidnapped everyone here. Also, Sujan was right—you suck," I retorted, which I think made him a bit sad at the mention of Sujan.

"Do not bring him up!" Narad demanded.

"Or what? You would do the same thing you did with his mom to us?" Ria replied, angrily.

Her voice had gone cold in a way I had never heard from her. Whatever Narad had done to Sujan's mom, Ria had clearly been there for the aftermath.

And then Narad looked at her, realised something, and said, "I remember you. You were there that night, with that daughter of Vishnu, when I visited Sujan on the streets."

I then saw Aryan take something from Narad's pockets without him noticing, and I grabbed both Aryan and Ria by their hands—so this time we wouldn't lose each other—and ran.

I managed to drag them with me, but because I couldn't find the door we had entered through, I took the stairs, and we entered the car parking or something like that.

Then Aryan showed us what he had taken from Narad: some sort of car keys.

I took the keys from Aryan's hand, pressed the "open doors" button, and ran towards the sound.

Ria found the note on the windshield, took it out, and read, "Clever, stealing from the god of thieves, messengers, and travelers."

He knew we had stolen from him, but I didn't care.

We got inside Narad's car, and since I am the only one who kind of knows the mortal world better, I sat in the driver's seat. Though everyone was panicking for some reason.

I started the car and accelerated forward. I bumped into the wall while taking it out of the building.

Behind us, I could hear Narad laughing. Not angry laughing. Genuinely entertained laughing. Somehow, that was worse.
