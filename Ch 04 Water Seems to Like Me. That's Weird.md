# Chapter 4: Water Seems to Like Me. That's Weird.

After I said bye to Ria, I went back to find Sujan, as it was about time for my fighting lessons with him.
I went to the secluded place near the forest where I liked to train with him, and there he was, always on time. We never liked training at the sparring arena.

I took out my ballpoint pen and uncapped it to let it take the form of a trident again. At this point I was really comfortable with it. Sujan took out his sword. He is a really good swordsman at camp—no one has ever defeated him.

He explained to me that we demigods use celestial bronze weapons; it's the only thing that can hurt monsters and gods alike. But us demigods can be hurt by both mortal weapons and celestial bronze, as we demigods walk two worlds.

He and I started our duel. I was still no match for him, but at least this time I gave him a little challenge. He landed a powerful blow, which I blocked with the tip of my trident, and I used its backside to push him away from me without hurting him. But eventually he became overwhelming with his incredible speed and knocked me down.

We sat down on a rock and panted a little.

"You're getting faster," Sujan said. "Last week you would have been on the ground in ten seconds."

"Why do the gods work this way? I mean, a system of claiming their kids and all?" I asked.

"What do you do when you see something so small and terrifying? Like a spider? You squish it, right?" he said. "We demigods are like that to them. The gods are bound by the ancient law and can't interfere with mortal affairs. But we—we are not bound by any ancient law. Which makes us far more dangerous and unpredictable."

…

Anyway, we walked back towards camp, and on the way back I bumped into some girl, and she pushed me down.

"Oh, you are the new kid, huh? The one who beat that Mahishasura?" Sanvi said, with a smirk on her face.

"Sanvi, leave him alone." Sujan moved forward to cover me, and the girl left. Then Sujan gave me a hand to pull me back up.

"Who was she, and what was her problem?" I asked.

"She is Sanvi, a daughter of Kartikeya, the god of war, and also the head of the Kartikeya cabin," Sujan replied. "Don't mind her. We demigods live for glory. And seeing you beat that Mahishasura must have made her feel overshadowed."

"Glory?" I asked.

"Yeah, glory is what we thrive for. Doing something no one has ever done before," Sujan replied.

"So you are saying that if I continue to do such stuff, my father would have no choice but to claim me?" I asked.

"Yeah, kinda," he replied.

Later that day, I visited Varuni at the big house.

"Oh, there you are, Swarn," Varuni said, softly.

"Oh, hey, uhm, what did you want me for?" I replied nervously.

"Oh, don't be nervous around me, Swarn. I am not like my fellow gods," Varuni said. "Besides, I wanted to tell you about tomorrow's activities," she added.

"Tomorrow's activities? What's gonna happen tomorrow?" I asked.

"Oh, it's tradition around camp to host Capture the Flag whenever a new demigod joins us. So I wanted to make sure I knew which team you would join. It's mostly two cabins who always take charge of the two teams—Kartikeya's and Saraswati's," she said.

"Why do those two cabins lead the two teams?" I asked.

"It's the rivalry between them. One thinks brute strength is required to win the war, and the other thinks you also need strategies," Varuni replied.

Well, I had just met Sanvi, who is the head of the Kartikeya cabin, so I didn't think I should join her side.

"I would like to be a part of the Saraswati team," I said, slowly.

Which made Varuni smile, and she said, "Alright then. Now go rest for tomorrow."

…

On my way back, I encountered Sanvi again, and I smiled at her and her friends and said, "Hey, guys!"

"You will, without any complaint, admit that you didn't kill Mahishasura and that you are a liar," Sanvi said, with a scoff on her face.

And her friends grabbed me and started dragging me towards the toilet.

There were four of them. I thrashed the whole way down the corridor, but they were all bigger than me, and camp kids literally train for this stuff. They shoved me through the bathroom door, and the smell of cheap cleaning liquid hit me, and someone forced my head down towards the toilet seat. The porcelain was cold against my cheek. I had been here before—not this bathroom, but this exact moment, in other schools, with other Alisons. And something in me finally said: no. Not this time.

As they started trying to push my head onto the toilet seat, I could feel the same thing I had felt when I seemingly made the water from the fountain grab Alison Hogan at the museum, but this time it was more intense. It felt like dancing to me, though—the more aggressive form of it. It started in my chest and rolled outwards, and the water answered it like it had been waiting for permission.

The water erupted out of the toilet seat, avoiding me, and drenched all of them. I am not exaggerating—it curved around me, like I was a stone in a river, and it hit Sanvi and her friends like a wave. And they ran away cursing, slipping on the wet floor the whole way out.

I stood there, completely dry. Not one drop had touched me. I stared at my hands, then at the water settling back down like nothing had happened, and I didn't know what scared me more: that I had done it, or that it had felt so good.

I turned around and noticed that Ria had just come in and witnessed everything, which surprised her too, and she started thinking again as she turned away—which, at this point, I knew meant she needed alone time to create her theories.

…
