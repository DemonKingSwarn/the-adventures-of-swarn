# Chapter 11: I Went to Meet My Dad and Fought a God Instead

“What just happened?” Ria asked, looking a bit stunned.

“I prayed to Diti. He said gods can’t help us, so I asked for her help instead.” I said proudly.

“Damn dude, now I am actually glad we met them on the train.” Aryan said.

“So Diti was telling us the truth about her blessing.” Ria said, grudgingly admitting Diti told the truth.

For a moment, we just lay there on the forest floor, breathing. My ears were still ringing from the flash. The last thing I had seen before we disappeared was my mom, frozen in gold, and now I had left her behind in Patal. The deal with Yama was the only thing keeping me from running straight back there.

“So where are we?” I asked while looking to find clues on where are we.

Then we hear a clapping sound with approaching footsteps.

We looked at the sound’s direction and it was coming from a man in a leather motorcycle jacket with a military-cut hairstyle. He had a slightly darker complexion.

“Well well look who is here. The one who broke my daughter’s spear which I gifted her, my half-brother.” Said the man looking straight at me.

Wait her daughter’s spear? Then I remembered breaking Sanvi’s spear during Capture the Flag and it clicked that this man is Kartikeya.

I looked at both Ria and Aryan and mumbled quietly “Why am I meeting every god who has a grudge against me.”

I looked at Kartikeya right in the eye and said “Come on, can’t you let it slide?”

Even though I pleaded, I instinctively took out my ballpoint pen and finally uncapped it. I really thought I wouldn’t need to use it in this quest, how naive of me.

Judging by Kartikeya’s reaction to me taking out my trident, I think he expected it because he just smirked and took out his own sword.

“You use the same weapon as our father, huh?” Kartikeya said.

I didn’t respond to him at first and then I don’t know what possessed me that I challenged him.

“I challenge you for a one on one combat.” I said.

I had enough of these gods coming in my way. So what he is the literal god of war.

Ria and Aryan looked at each other and then Ria said “Are you sure you wanna fight him? He is strong, and could k…kill you.”

“You asked for death, kid.” Kartikeya said.

“First one to bleed loses.” I said.

We took our positions.

For a second, nobody moved. This wasn't Sanvi showing off during Capture the Flag. This was the god of war, and there was no Vajivega waiting to blow a shell and end the fight.

He struck first, which I blocked using the trick Sujan taught me. By making him believe I was wide open and then using the back side of the trident to block his sword.

The force of the blow went all the way up my arms. Sujan's training was the only reason I was still standing. Kartikeya wasn't even breathing hard.

Then he lifted me up with one arm and then threw me in the distance, and then approached me and kicked me on the chest.

I hit the ground hard enough to see white. For a moment, I couldn't breathe at all. I could hear Ria shouting my name, but it sounded far away. My trident was still in my hand, though I didn't remember holding onto it.

But then he made a mistake, he made me angry. That’s when I got the same sensation on my forehead I got at camp. My moves started becoming like I was dancing.

The pain didn't disappear. I just stopped listening to it. All the fear I had carried through Patal, all the helplessness I had felt seeing my mom on that throne, turned into something I could actually use.

And then I slid right between his legs and made him bleed in his left leg. The blood coming out was golden.

For a heartbeat, I just stared at it. I had expected the fight to keep going. Some part of me had expected to die. Instead, there was golden blood on the ground, and Kartikeya was looking at me like he was seeing me properly for the first time.

“Tejas! The blood of the gods.” Aryan remarked, gasping.

Both of them immediately ran towards me.

“You just defeated a god.” Ria said with awe.

I didn't feel like I had won anything. My whole body was shaking, now that the dancing sensation was leaving it. I had challenged him because I was tired of gods getting in my way. Now that it was over, I mostly felt tired.

And then Kartikeya began glowing with gold light.

“Look away, he is taking his true form, we can't look at a god's true form without getting burned to ashes.” Aryan said.

We looked away and closed our eyes. When the light was gone and we looked back, Kartikeya wasn’t there.

Where Kartikeya was, now remains the armour. I went to check it and held it, that's when I see Mrs Shristi come back.

It took me a second to understand what I was holding. Yama's stolen armour. The thing he had sent us to find. After everything—the fight, the light, Kartikeya disappearing—that part of the quest had ended up right there in my hands.

"I will take it from here." She said as she took the armour from my hands and flew off.

I watched her until she was gone. I could only hope that getting the armour back meant Yama would keep his word about my mom.

“We should go! Thanks to Diti, we are already at Dandakaranya's entry.” Ria said.

“Guys wait. The prophecy said no demigod has returned from Dandakaranya. You two should head back to camp to deliver the bone blade back to Danu and Diti. I should go alone from here, it’s part of my father’s domain.” I said, pleading to them.

“What? We can’t just leave you.” said by both Ria and Aryan in unison.

“Please guys, I promise I will return from there. But you two have to go back.” I said.

“Are you sure?” Ria asked, nervously.

I nod.

Ria looked like she wanted to argue again. Instead, she reached for her necklace. That almost made me take back what I had said, because I knew what it meant to her.

“Alright, if you are sure, then here have this for good luck.” Ria said as she handed me the bead necklace she got from camp. Each bead shows each year she spent at camp since coming there at 7 years old.

It wasn't just a necklace. It was every year she had survived away from home, every year at camp, and she was putting all of it in my hands.

Aryan followed suit with the can of bhaang he always carries.

The bhaang can was so perfectly Aryan that I almost laughed. Somehow, that made it worse.

We three hugged tightly.

None of us let go straight away. I kept thinking of all the times I had been annoyed by Aryan's singing, or wished Ria would stop thinking for just one minute. I would have given anything for one more ordinary afternoon of that.

When we finally separated, I made myself watch them leave. A small, scared part of me wanted to run after them and tell them I had changed my mind. The prophecy was the only reason I didn't.

…

As soon as they left, I started walking towards the Dandakaranya.

It was strange how loud the silence became. There was no Aryan complaining about being hungry, no Ria working through a theory out loud. Just my footsteps and the necklace in my pocket. Every few steps, I had to stop myself from looking back.

As I approached the Dandakaranya, the winged shoes slowly started getting activated. I didn’t give it much thought at first.

Then I heard the voice, the same voice of the hooded man. He said “Bring him to me, bring the boy to me.”

When I opened my eyes and came out of trance, I noticed I was flying using those very winged shoes, right above the some sort of giant giant pit on the ground.

And then the winged shoes started taking me right towards the pit, so I quickly uncapped my ballpoint pen again and let go of the shoes and used the trident to safely land on the ground, while the shoes get flown into the pit.

I stayed crouched on the ground for a moment, waiting for my heart to slow down. A few more seconds and I would have gone in with them. I looked at the pit, then at the empty place on my feet where Sujan's gift had been. I didn't want to think about what that meant yet.

And then I remembered that one line of the prophecy, “The god who has turned.” It was Kartikeya who was doing this.

This pit was never meant to be here, and somehow its here.

I looked around and then see the same two people I saw at the bus stand way back. They smiled at me and the woman spoke "You are brave, young one."

"Who are you?" I asked, a bit nervous.

"Who are we? We are the fate itself, We are Vidhi and Vidhata." Said the Vidhata. "You have a long way to go." Vidhi added.

And they both disappeared in a flash of blinding light.

I had so many questions. Why had they been watching me at the bus stand? What did they mean by a long way to go? But the light left me alone again before I could ask even one of them.

And when I opened my eyes, I was standing at the mountain top, and then noticed my father Shiva standing right in front of me, smiling.

He was wearing a simple mountaineer’s outfit, with hair tied into a bun.

“You have grown quite well.” Shiva said. “I was hoping to finally meet you, my son.”

He then embraces me in a hug.

I just looked at him, like I wasn’t expecting this.

I had imagined meeting my father so many times. In every version, I was angry. I demanded to know where he had been, why he had left my mom alone, why it had taken monsters and a quest for him to finally appear. But now that he was holding me, all of those speeches disappeared. I hugged him back and hated how badly I had needed to.

For a little while, I wasn't a demigod on a quest. I was just a kid whose dad had finally shown up.

“I still have unfinished business in the quest.” I mumbled softly.

“Yama’s contract? He has kept his end of the bargain, he doesn’t lie.” Shiva said.

“So he freed my mother? Is she back at home?” I asked.

Shiva just nods and smiles at me.

The breath went out of me. After seeing her frozen in Yama's throne room, after leaving her behind, she was safe. She was home. I wanted to run to her right then. I also wanted to stay exactly where I was, because I had only just found my father and he already felt a little like home too.

“You have your mother’s eyes.” Shiva said. “It’s time for you to go now.”

There were a hundred things I still wanted to ask him. Would I see him again? Had he been watching? Did he know how much I had needed him before this? But his arms were around me again, and for once I let the questions wait.

He embraced me one last time before the waters engulfed me and then I opened my eyes right in front of the Astrid’s peepal tree at camp.

I walked back to the camp, the other demigods clapped for me as I completed my quest. And then Ria approached me and just hugged me tightly. And I hugged her back.

I held on for a moment. She was really here. They had both made it back. Over her shoulder, I saw Aryan watching us with the biggest grin on his face, and the tightness in my chest finally started to loosen.

The Kartikeya cabin campers, especially Sanvi were not happy with my return. They probably heard the news that I defeated their father.

“Have you returned the bone blade to Danu and Diti?” I whispered to Ria.

Ria just nodded.

I eventually got into the big house, finally meeting Varuni and Vajivega again. They were playing their game of Chausar again. Varuni was playing her casual sundress as she always wore, while Vajivega was wearing his party clothes.

Varuni notices me and smiles.
